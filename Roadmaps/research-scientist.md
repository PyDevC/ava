# Research Scientist (ML)

Research Scientists work on **advancing the field**: new architectures, algorithms, training methods, and analyses. Success = publications, reproducible experiments, and novel findings — not shipping features.

## Core skills

- **Deep ML theory + practice**: can derive and implement new methods from papers. Reading literature fluently (arXiv, NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR).
- **Strong math**: linear algebra, probability/stats, optimization, information theory.
- **PyTorch** at research-grade level: training loops, custom layers, distributed training (DDP/FSDP).
- **Experimental rigor**: ablations, baselines, seeds, statistical significance, correct evaluation (see [ranking](../MachineLearning/algorithms/ranking.md) for metric subtleties).
- **Scientific writing + presenting**: papers, posters, talks.

## Typical day-to-day

- Reading papers → forming a hypothesis → implementing a method → running large experiments → iterating → writing it up.
- Lots of debugging training runs, fighting infra, and analyzing why experiments fail.
- Mentoring, code review, reviews for conferences.

## Roadmap to get there

1. BSc-level math + a solid ML course (from scratch, not just libraries).
2. Read and reimplement 10–20 classic papers (AlexNet→ResNet, Transformer, BERT/GPT, diffusion...).
3. Contribute to or run your own experiments in PyTorch ([Explore](../PyTorch/Explore.md)).
4. Deep-dive into one area (NLP, CV, RL, optimization) — see my notes in [MachineLearning](../MachineLearning/PLAN.md).
5. PhD or equivalent deep-project experience → publications.
